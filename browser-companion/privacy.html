Browser import privacy & use

Effective October 26, 2026. Production companion 1.0.1.

You choose the page

The companion runs only after you click Read this recipe. Chrome's activeTab permission grants temporary access to that tab. There are no background scripts, automatic crawling, or persistent access to all websites.

What it reads

The current page's title, source URL, Recipe metadata, and recipe area. It excludes form controls, editable fields, common navigation and comment areas. It does not read cookies, passwords, browsing history, or other tabs. Website layouts vary: review the captured content before saving.

Where it goes

The companion reads accessible pixels of a selected food photo already on the page, or requests a same-site HTTPS image without cookies or redirects. It has no analytics, advertising, or cloud upload. Protected cross-site photos may be unavailable; the recipe file explains when you need to paste a photo manually. The recipe and selected photo (when readable), including its source URL, are saved as a local JSON file you choose to import into Nibble. Your browser still connects to the website normally under that site's privacy policy.

Storage and removal

Nibble stores recipes, source evidence, and import drafts on your device. Downloads, backups, exports, and copies synced by your operating system must be removed separately. Moving a recipe to Trash does not erase its import history. Remove the extension through your browser's Extensions page to revoke access.

Respect the source

Use pages you can access normally for your own recipe collection. This companion does not unlock restricted content or defeat access checks. Preserve creator attribution and the source link. Importing a recipe does not grant permission to republish it. Review ingredients and nutrition; imported values are not independently verified.

Optional Nibble connections

This policy describes browser imports. Nibble's direct link importer contacts the selected website. Optional USDA lookups send food searches to USDA; optional local Ollama processing sends supplied recipe text to the model running on your computer. Browser imports themselves require neither service.
